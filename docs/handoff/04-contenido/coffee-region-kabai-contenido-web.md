## 02 · The Coffee Region

*Notion: https://app.notion.com/p/3eb1d15d036a81d8ae41cc978b7cd07f*

**Título**

A land that holds you.

**Texto**

Here, in the heart of Colombia, the mountains fold into one another, wax palms rise above the mist, water runs down every slope, and the scent of coffee reaches you before the farm does. The essentials are cared for, the climate is gentle, and people welcome you the way you are welcomed at home. Something in the body understands it early: here, you can let your guard down.

Kabai is an Emberá Chamí word — the Emberá Chamí are an Indigenous community of Colombia's western Andes — that holds two meanings at once: to know and to work, especially the land. For us, it names a way of travelling: coming to know a place by taking part in it, not by looking at it from a distance. Over seven days between Quindío and Risaralda, the journey moves and pauses, with time to walk, to learn with your hands, and to rest in between. There is nothing to collect here, only the feeling of a body that has been given time.

Arrival · Welcome dinner

**Detalle** (alojamiento · comidas · guía)

Boutique stay near Salento · welcome dinner

### Day 2

**Título**

Opening · The forest · Filandia

**Texto**

The journey opens with a guided ritual to arrive in body and mind. Then a morning walk through a private reserve, where native forest shelters howler monkeys and abundant birdlife; they are most active early, so mornings bring the best chance to see them. Lunch in Filandia is yours to choose, among places we've handpicked, followed by its painted streets and an afternoon with a master weaver who teaches the town's young people to turn vine into baskets, so the craft does not end with him.

**Detalle** (alojamiento · comidas · guía)

Boutique stay near Salento · breakfast · private English-speaking guide

### Day 3

**Título**

The wax palms

**Texto**

A day in the Cocora valley, on a private farm that protects its palm forest. You walk up with the people who care for the land, past native trees and open views, to where the wax palms rise above the mist. There is no rush here: the hilltop is for staying. In the late afternoon, an arepa workshop at the hotel brings you closer to corn, one of the most essential ingredients of the Colombian table: you grind it by hand, shape your own arepas, then sit down to a generous tasting of five regional styles.

**Detalle** (alojamiento · comidas · guía)

Boutique stay near Salento · breakfast, lunch, arepa workshop and tasting (a meal in itself) · private English-speaking guide

### Day 4

**Título**

A pause · Salento · On to Risaralda

**Texto**

Midway through, a morning to pause: a guided practice, time to reflect on what the days have stirred, and the hotel's waterfall close by. Later, a last unhurried walk through Salento, lunch wherever you like, and the mountain road down to Risaralda, with a stop where the coffee valleys open below.

**Detalle** (alojamiento · comidas · guía)

Boutique stay near Pereira · breakfast

### Day 5

**Título**

Spa · Clay or kitchen

**Texto**

A spa morning with no clock, and time to enjoy the hotel: built from guadua and natural wood, wrapped in gardens, with a kitchen worth staying in for. In the afternoon, choose your way into the region's crafts: a clay workshop among the old kilns of a brickworks in a nearby town, or a family kitchen in the hills at 1,900 metres, cooking from the garden and sharing the meal at their table.

**Detalle** (alojamiento · comidas · guía)

Boutique stay near Pereira · breakfast, lunch

### Day 6

**Título**

Marsella · Closing fire

**Texto**

The road leads to Marsella, the town that bought the land where its water is born. After a walk through its streets, a coffee-growing family opens their farm to you and walks you through coffee from seed to cup: you plant a seed of your own, walk the rows, and watch the ripe cherries stripped of their pulp and the beans laid out to dry. Then lunch at their table, cooked by the farmer herself: a true country meal of beans, plantain and rice, made with ingredients grown on the farm or close by. That night, the journey closes around a fire set up just for you.

**Detalle** (alojamiento · comidas · guía)

Boutique stay near Pereira · breakfast, lunch, closing bonfire · private English-speaking guide

### Day 7

**Título**

Farewell

**Texto**

A last coffee-region morning, breakfast without hurry, and your private transfer to the airport.

**Detalle** (alojamiento · comidas · guía)

Departure · breakfast

## 11 · Good to know

*Notion: https://app.notion.com/p/3eb1d15d036a81179a70dea39896077e*

**Título**

Answers to the questions we hear most, before you say yes.

### Pregunta 1

**Pregunta**

Is the coffee region safe to travel?

**Respuesta**

Yes. It's one of Colombia's most established places to travel, with good roads, a long tradition of hosting visitors and a calm, rural pace. You move with private drivers and guides we know personally, and we're a message away before and during the journey.

### Pregunta 2

**Pregunta**

How does a private journey work?

**Respuesta**

It's yours alone: you choose the dates and who you travel with. After a first conversation, we confirm hotels and experiences for your dates and shape the open moments of the itinerary around what interests you most, from your menus to how you spend your free afternoons. The price shown is per person, based on two people sharing a room, and your final price comes with your proposal. Solo travellers are welcome; we'll share a single-room price when we talk.

### Pregunta 3

**Pregunta**

Is this journey right for me?

**Respuesta**

If you enjoy walking a couple of hours at an easy pace, yes. Some trails climb and descend and can be muddy after rain, so good shoes matter more than fitness. Every day leaves time to rest, and every walk can be shortened to suit you.

### Pregunta 4

**Pregunta**

When's the best time to go?

**Respuesta**

Any time of year. December to March and July to August tend to be drier; April, May, October and November bring more rain, usually in the afternoon, with clearer mornings. From September to December, the main coffee harvest is under way. Days are mild, around 18–25 °C (64–77 °F), and evenings turn cool in the mountains.

### Pregunta 5

**Pregunta**

How do I get there?

**Respuesta**

Fly into Armenia (AXM) or Pereira (PEI), usually with a short connection through Bogotá or Medellín. Private transfers from and to the airport are included, and we'll suggest the best airport for your flights.

### Pregunta 6

**Pregunta**

Can I make the journey shorter or longer?

**Respuesta**

The seven days are designed as a whole, with a thread that runs from the first morning to the last night and a rhythm that needs that time, so we keep them together. If you have more time, the journey can continue: each extension adds two nights in another corner of the coffee region, and you can join more than one. Every stay and experience in them we've already chosen and tried, from a historic coffee hacienda to a hotel high in the mountains, close to the snow-capped volcanoes. Tell us when we talk, and we'll include it in your proposal.

And then, there are the condors.

Nothing in the wild can be guaranteed. But here, you can wake up, look out across one of the most extraordinary views of the journey, and know that a condor may appear above the valley.
