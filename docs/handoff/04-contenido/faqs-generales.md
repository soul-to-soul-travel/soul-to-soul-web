# Before You Travel

### When to Travel

Colombia works year-round. There is no winter: temperature is set by altitude, not by season, so a mountain morning is cool in July and cool in January.

What changes is rain.

- **December to April** — driest months on the Caribbean coast. The best window for the Sierra Nevada.
- **June to August** — clear mornings and sunny afternoons in the mountains. The best window for the Coffee Region.
- **September to November** — more rain across the country, greener landscapes, fewer travelers.

Rain is part of the mountains in any month. It rarely lasts all day, and we plan around it.

### Payments & Booking

Choose whichever method is easiest for you. Once it's done, send us a confirmation so we can secure your dates.

**By card** — credit or debit, through the link we share with you.

**By bank transfer** — Wise or a US local transfer (ACH), usually with lower fees. Tell us and we'll walk you through it.

**Other options** — Remitly, international wire (SWIFT) or crypto. We can guide you through any of these.

### Passport & Travel Requirements

- A passport valid for at least **6 months** beyond your travel dates.
- At least **2 blank pages** — 4 is more comfortable.
- A **return or onward ticket**. This one matters: without proof of exit you may be asked to buy one on the spot, or be denied entry.
- **Digital copies** of your passport and key documents, somewhere easy to reach.

**On visas:** many nationalities enter Colombia for tourism without a visa, for stays of up to 90 days. Requirements depend on your passport and they change, so check the official Migración Colombia site for your nationality before you book your flights — and tell us if anything about your situation is unusual, so we can help you prepare.

### Travel Documents Checklist

- [ ] Valid passport (6+ months, 2 blank pages)
- [ ] Travel insurance (required)

- [ ] Your itinerary and accommodation details
- [ ] Credit and debit cards — ideally 2, with no foreign transaction fees

### Flights & Airports

**International flights are not included** in your journey, and we don't issue them. You're free to book the route that suits you, and we'll tell you which airport and which arrival window works for your itinerary.

Most travelers fly into **Bogotá (BOG)**, and many add two or three days there before the journey begins.

From Bogotá, a short domestic flight connects to the region:

- **Armenia or Pereira** for the Coffee Region
- **Santa Marta** for the Sierra Nevada

**Domestic flights within Colombia** are not in the base price, but we can arrange and include them in your package — just ask.

### Before You Fly

A few small things that save big headaches:

- Tell your bank you'll be traveling, and enable international use on your cards.
- Save digital copies of your passport, insurance and reservations.
- Download **WhatsApp**. It's how we, your guides, your drivers and the hotels stay in touch with you.
- Download offline maps of the areas you'll visit, in case you're without signal.

# Entry & Immigration

### Entry Requirements & Check-Mig

**Check-Mig is mandatory.** Complete the online form between **72 hours and 1 hour before** each flight into and out of Colombia.

- Form: [Check-Mig — Migración Colombia](https://apps.migracioncolombia.gov.co/pre-registro/en)

**At immigration, you may be asked for:**

- The purpose of your trip
- Where you're staying
- How long you're staying
- Your return or onward ticket

> Tourists can usually stay up to **90 days**. Working on a tourist entry isn't permitted.

### Staying Longer — Visa Extension

Want to stay beyond your first 90 days? You can request a **Temporary Stay Permit (PTP)**.

**The key rule:** apply *before* your 90 days run out — ideally at least **5 business days** ahead. If your days lapse first, you enter irregular status and may face fines or restrictions.

**What it gives you**

- Up to **90 additional days**.
- A maximum of **180 days total within any rolling 12 months** — not a calendar year.

**How to apply**

1. Go to the official Migración Colombia website.
2. Complete the FUT form online.
3. Upload your passport, entry stamp and travel support.
4. Wait for pre-approval.
5. Pay the fee (around 150,000 COP).
6. Receive your permit by email, usually within 3 business days.

> If you overstay, you can't apply normally — you'd pay a fine on exit, request a special permit in limited cases, or leave the country. When in doubt, ask us early.

### Vaccines

**No vaccine is required to enter Colombia.** What we recommend:

- **Yellow fever** — not required to enter Colombia, and not something every journey calls for. It is recommended for the Amazon and Orinoco regions, the jungle areas of the Caribbean, and certain lowland departments. Separately, some regional authorities ask to see the vaccination card at the entrance to protected areas and natural reserves, and a ranger at a gate is not somewhere to discover you don't have it. If your itinerary includes a reserve or a natural park, we'll tell you — and where it applies, get vaccinated at least **10 days before** you travel and bring the international certificate with you.
- **Tetanus** — worth having up to date.
- **Routine vaccines** — up to date, as you would for any trip.

### Traveling with Minors

**A child traveling alone** needs a notarized authorization from both parents — airlines require it and immigration may ask for it.

**A child traveling with one parent** needs an authorization letter from the non-traveling parent.

> Without the right paperwork, a minor can be denied boarding or held at immigration. If this applies to you, tell us ahead of time and we'll help you prepare.

# Money in Colombia

### Cash, Cards & Tipping

Colombia isn't cash-only, but it isn't cashless either. The sweet spot is a mix of both.

**How much cash to bring**

For a two-week trip, plan for roughly **USD 700–1,000 in cash** to cover taxis, tips, small restaurants, markets and rural areas.

**A good balance**

- Cards: around 70–80% of your spending.
- Cash: around 20–30%.
- Don't carry it all at once — use the hotel safe.

**Where cards work well**

- Hotels, mid- and high-end restaurants, malls, and apps like Uber and Rappi.
- Bring at least **2 cards**, ideally with no international fees. Machines occasionally fail, and some places take only local cards.

**Where you'll want cash**

- Small shops and markets, taxis outside apps, tips, small tours, and rural areas.

**Changing money and ATMs**

- Exchange only USD 50–100 at the airport. Change the rest in the city, at banks, ATMs or exchange houses.
- ATMs are widely available. Withdrawal fees run around **USD 3–8** depending on the bank.
- You may enter Colombia with up to USD 10,000 without declaring it.

**Regions to plan cash for**

- **Sierra Nevada** — mostly no ATMs and no card machines. Carry enough cash.
- **Coffee Region** — exchange is available in Pereira, Armenia and Manizales, but rural areas and small towns run on cash.

**Tipping**

- Restaurants: around 10%, usually offered as a service charge. It's optional.
- Guides and drivers: not expected, always appreciated. Around USD 5–10 per service is a good reference.
- Hotels: small tips are welcome.

> Common slip-ups: arriving with no cash, changing everything at the airport, relying only on contactless, or carrying all your cash at once.

# Packing

### Luggage & What to Pack

**Domestic flight luggage**

Checked up to 20 kg · carry-on 7 kg · one personal item.

**The general idea: layers.** Colombia's climate is set by altitude, and a single journey can move through more than one of them. Rather than packing for one temperature, pack for two or three and combine.

**Everywhere**

- Light, comfortable clothing you can move in
- A light jacket or sweater for cool mornings and evenings
- Comfortable walking shoes
- Swimsuit
- Sunscreen, insect repellent, a hat or cap, sunglasses
- Any personal medication you need

**Mountains — Coffee Region, Bogotá**

- Light layers, plus a warm sweater for the evenings
- Walking shoes that can get muddy and wet. Trails in the Coffee Region are earth, not pavement, and after rain they stay soft for a while.
- A second pair of shoes. Mountain air doesn't dry a wet pair overnight.

**Caribbean coast — Sierra Nevada**

- Light, breathable clothing
- Long sleeves and long pants for the evenings — the best mosquito protection there is
- Sandals or river shoes, plus walking shoes

> **What we provide, so you don't pack it:** a rain poncho on journeys where the itinerary calls for one, and a reusable water bottle in your RAÍZ travel kit. Where an experience needs specific gear — binoculars for birdwatching, rubber boots for a particular walk — it's arranged for you. Your journey's own packing notes will tell you what's specific to it.

### Electrical Outlets

Colombia runs on **110V, 60Hz**, with **Type A and Type B** plugs — the flat two-pin and three-pin sockets.

Check your devices against that. If your plugs or your voltage are different, bring an adapter, and a converter for anything that isn't dual-voltage. Most phone and laptop chargers handle both; hair dryers, straighteners and small appliances often don't.

# On the Ground

### Connectivity & Communication

**Getting online** — roaming (easy but pricey), a local SIM from Claro or Tigo, or an **e-SIM**. We recommend the e-SIM; Airalo works well.

**WhatsApp is essential.** It's how guides, drivers, hotels and our team reach you, and you don't need a Colombian number to use it.

**Wi-Fi** is reliable at the hotels we work with. In remote nature areas the signal gets thinner, which is part of being there.

**Language** — Spanish is the main language, and English is common in tourism but not outside it. Your guides and hosts are bilingual, so you're never left without a way to communicate. A *por favor* and a *gracias* go a long way.

### Drinking Water

Tap water is safe to drink in Bogotá and in the Coffee Region. On the Caribbean coast it isn't — stick to purified water there.

Purified water is available at the hotels and during activities, and your travel kit includes a reusable bottle.

### Food & Dietary Needs

Colombian food is fresh, generous and regional. Expect fruit you've never seen, soups that are a whole meal, and coffee that tastes like where it grew.

Vegetarian and gluten-free are straightforward almost everywhere. Strict vegan is manageable in hotels and cities, and can get harder at a family farm or in a small town, where the menu is simply what the house cooks that day.

Tell us your restrictions when you book, not when you arrive. We coordinate them in advance with every hotel and host on your itinerary, and we'll tell you honestly if a particular meal can't be adapted.

### Health & Well-being

- Bring any personal medication you need, in its original packaging, and a copy of the prescription for anything unusual.
- Travel insurance is **required**. Keep your policy number and the emergency line where you can find them quickly.
- Pack sunscreen and insect repellent. In the mountains the sun is stronger than it feels, especially on a cloudy day.

**Mosquitoes** — present and persistent in the Sierra Nevada, where long sleeves in the evening are the best defense. Fewer in the Coffee Region, but repellent is still worth packing.

**If something happens during your journey** — tell us, at any hour. We'll help you reach care: the hotels we work with can call a doctor, the regional capitals have good private clinics, and your insurance is what covers the cost, which is exactly why we require it. For anything serious, the emergency number in Colombia is **123**.

### Altitude

Altitude is worth understanding, because it shapes both the temperature and how your body feels.

Discomfort from altitude generally starts above **2,500 m (8,200 ft)**, and it can show up as a headache, light dizziness, poor sleep or shortness of breath on a climb. Bogotá sits at 2,600 m. Mountain itineraries can pass above that line on a given walk.

If you're coming from sea level, the first day is the one to take gently: drink more water than usual, go easy on alcohol, and don't plan your hardest walk for the morning after you land. Most people adjust within a day or two. If symptoms get worse instead of better, say so — going lower is the remedy, and it works quickly.

If you have a heart or lung condition, or you're pregnant, talk to your doctor about altitude before you book.

### Laundry

Most hotels offer laundry for an additional cost, usually back the same or next day. On a journey with more than one hotel, it's easiest to send it at the first one.

### Photography & Drones

Photograph freely in most places. Two exceptions worth respecting:

- **People.** Ask before photographing someone, especially in Indigenous communities and small towns. Sometimes the answer is no, and your host will tell you when that's the case.
- **Drones.** Allowed in many areas, but not inside Indigenous territories or certain natural parks and private reserves. Ask your host before you fly one.

### Safety & General Tips

The regions we work in — the Coffee Region, the Sierra Nevada and selected neighborhoods of Bogotá — are well-developed for international visitors, and we move through them with drivers and guides we know personally.

The ordinary rules apply, as they would in any city in the world: stay aware of your surroundings, don't display valuables, use the transport we arrange or a trusted app rather than hailing a taxi off the street, and keep your documents and cash in the hotel safe.

**Traveling alone?** Our journeys are private, so you're never put in a group of strangers. You'll have a bilingual guide on the days the itinerary includes one, drivers we trust on every transfer, and our team reachable the whole way through. Many of our travelers come on their own, women included.

# About the Journeys

### How our journeys work

**Every RAÍZ journey is private and on demand.** We don't form groups: you choose your dates and you travel with whoever you want to travel with — alone, as a couple, with family or with friends. Prices are built per person in double occupancy, and we quote a single traveler or a larger group on request.

We design each journey around a territory and the people in it, with enough space built in to rest, wander and let the days land. It isn't a checklist of attractions, and it isn't a retreat with a fixed schedule either.

**Our support is remote.** We're not standing beside you for seven days. You'll have local guides on the days the itinerary includes them, hosts who receive you at each experience, and us — reachable before and during the journey, coordinating with hotels and hosts on your behalf, at any hour.

### How physically demanding are they?

Our journeys are made for people of ordinary fitness, and almost nothing in them is strenuous. But "a walk" means different things in different territories, so we rate every activity on the same five-level scale, and your itinerary tells you which level each one is:

| Level | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Gentle | On your feet, or a short walk on flat ground. No sustained effort. |
| Gentle to Moderate | An easy walk with a short climb or descent along the way. |
| Moderate | One to three hours walking, uneven ground with some elevation, a steady pace. |
| Moderate to Challenging | A long walk with real elevation and sustained steep stretches. |
| Challenging | More than three hours, significant elevation, or above 2,500 m. |

If an activity doesn't suit you, say so. There's almost always another way to spend that morning, and asking for it costs you nothing.

### Accessibility

We want to be straight with you rather than optimistic. Our itineraries include walks in natural settings, uneven ground, stairs and places that are not wheelchair accessible — particularly natural reserves, national parks and Indigenous territories.

If mobility is a consideration for you, tell us early and in detail. Some journeys can be adapted more than others, and we'd rather design around it from the start than have you discover the limit on day three.

### Where you'll stay

Small boutique hotels, haciendas and ecolodges — chosen one by one, and always after we've slept there ourselves. What we look for is the same everywhere: honest architecture, a real connection to the landscape around it, care in the service, and a bed worth coming back to at the end of a long day.

Breakfast is included every morning. Your itinerary describes each place you'll sleep.

### What's included

Inclusions vary by journey, and your itinerary is the exact list. As a rule, a RAÍZ journey includes:

- **All ground transportation** from the moment you land until the moment you leave, including every transfer between towns and experiences.
- **Accommodation with daily breakfast.**
- **Every experience and entrance fee** listed in the itinerary.
- **Local guides** for the experiences that call for one, and a private bilingual guide on the days noted in your itinerary.
- **The meals tied to an experience** — a picnic, a tasting, a cooking class, a lunch at the farm — plus the welcome dinner.
- **Support before and during the journey**, coordinated with hotels and hosts on your behalf.

**Not included:** international flights; domestic flights (we can arrange them); travel insurance, which is required; the meals left open in your itinerary; optional add-on experiences; personal expenses and tips.

**On the open meals:** they're deliberate, not an omission. Choosing where to eat is part of being somewhere. Budget around **USD 80 per day** for the meals that aren't included, depending on how you like to eat.

### Arriving earlier or staying longer

Yes, and it's usually a good idea. Every journey has a **two-night extension** already designed, costed and ready to quote — a different place, with its accommodation and experiences already chosen.

Many travelers also add two or three days in Bogotá at the start. Tell us what you have in mind and we'll put it together.
